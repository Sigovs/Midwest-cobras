## 5 · Page grammar (`DNA36`)

**~~Split stage.~~ SINGLE PINNED STAGE — changed by Alex, 2026-08-26, and the
old grammar is struck through rather than deleted so the change stays legible.**

The instruction: *the whole scroll happens in one scene on the hero — every act,
every annotation, every line of copy — with no fold.* So nothing sits below the
hero. There is one room, one camera and four moments in it, and the scroll is
handed to the camera rather than to the page.

The split stage that used to be here was a held frame beside an editorial column.
That grammar cannot survive the instruction: a column beside a frame needs the
page to keep scrolling past it, and here nothing scrolls past anything.

**How it differs from the last build, restated for the new grammar.** index7 is
a *filmic one-shot* — nine pinned beats, one continuous camera, a disassembly
peak, and the copy rides the whole way. This is four COMPOSED FRAMES with a cut
between them: the camera settles into each one and the copy is never on screen
while it swings, which is the opposite of a continuous shot. index6 is a chaptered editorial with a fold. Fingerprint: act
count (4 against 9), the hold-cut-hold cadence, the closing and the signature all
differ.

## 6 · The signature move (`DNA37`)

**The word is in the room, and the car eclipses it.**
`COBRA` stands on a plane behind the vehicle, in the same space and the same
light, so the body physically crops the letterforms. As the camera moves the
occlusion changes and the word is read in fragments — never as a whole, never as
an overlay.

It is bespoke because it is not a parameter change: a DOM word behind an opaque
canvas is invisible and a DOM word above it is a caption. Only geometry standing
in the room produces the crop, and the crop is the device.

## 7 · The shot list (`DNA27`, `DNA50`)

**THE EDIT, AS BUILT.** Long composed holds, short decisive cuts. The gaps
between the holds are the cuts; the numbers are fractions of the pinned scroll,
and the pin is 198% of a viewport — 1782 px at 1600 × 900.

| | Pin range | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **ACT 01 — hold** | 0–18% | 321 px |
| transition 01→02 | 18–26% | 143 px |
| **ACT 02 — hold** | 26–44% | 321 px |
| transition 02→03 | 44–52% | 143 px |
| **ACT 03 — hold** | 52–70% | 321 px |
| transition 03→04 | 70–78% | 143 px |
| **ACT 04 — hold** | 78–100% | 392 px |

Measured on the pin, walking it one pixel at a time:

> **78.1% fully composed states · 21.5% transitions · 0.4% with no readable copy.**
>
> The 0.4% is **8 px** — the single crossover point where the outgoing headline
> has reached zero and the incoming one starts. A frame, not a state.

Inside a hold the camera does not move, the lighting is the state that
composition was lit for, and the copy is at full strength: pause anywhere in it
and the frame is finished. Inside a cut the camera travels the whole distance in
8% of the pin on a cubic in-out, which is short enough to read as an edit between
two shots rather than as a drift.

**Both earlier cadences are kept here because each was wrong in the opposite
direction, and the pair is the argument.**

*Version one* spent each act's slot as |hold 34%|travel 32%|hold 34%|. Measured,
**2040 of 2970 pinned pixels — 68.7% — changed nothing**, with 0.55 s of scrub
lag on top. Two thirds of the reader's own input produced a frozen picture, and
Alex said the scroll felt bad before any of it was measured.

*Version two* removed the plateaus entirely and eased the camera across every
pixel. That fixed the hand and broke the eye: **28.7% composed, 38.3% half-faded,
33.0% with no copy at all.** Seven tenths of the scroll was transition, and
stopping anywhere but four exact points gave a half-dissolved headline over a
turning car — the look of a configurator rather than of a photograph.

The proportion of held scroll is now HIGHER than version one's and it does not
feel stuck, which is the part worth remembering: version one's motionless runs
were 680 px each, and a hold here is 321 px. The length of any single motionless
run is what a hand feels; the percentage is what an eye feels.

| Act | Frame | The cut into it |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | low front three-quarter, camera at bumper height | — the page opens here |
| 02 | the flank from in front of it, sill and side pipe running away | **orbit +35 deg**, radius 6.4 to 7.8 m |
| 03 | rear three-quarter, car high and right | **orbit +140 deg**, round the right flank |
| 04 | release — wide front three-quarter, the camera stands back | **orbit -172 deg**, back the way it came, radius out to 8.7 m |

**Every act carries its own lighting state and its own word**, interpolated
during a cut and arriving at a stated value for the hold. The backing pool is
placed from the camera azimuth rather than parked in the world, the rim goes with
it, and a second weak pool under the body gives the contact shadow some value to
bite into. Measured at the tyres against the open floor in the same frame:
94 / 84 / 75 / 107 against 3 / 4 / 2 / 1.

FOV is fixed at 30 degrees in every landscape act — inside the 28–40 heroic band
(`DNA51`) — and is never animated, so the rule against animating position and FOV
together cannot be reached. Portrait is 32 degrees throughout, with the same
constancy.

**THE CAMERA TRAVELS ON AN ARC, NOT A LINE, AND THIS WAS CAUGHT IN THE BROWSER
RATHER THAN REASONED ABOUT.** Straight interpolation between two camera positions
is a chord, and a chord between two points on opposite sides of a car goes
through the car: the first cut from act 03 to act 04 put the reader inside the
cockpit, four centimetres from a windscreen wiper. Position is now carried in
polar coordinates about the car's own axis — radius, height and angle, the angle
taking the shorter way round — which is how a camera on a jib actually moves.
Measured across the whole scroll, the closest the camera comes to the car's axis
is **6.42 m** against a half-length of 1.98 m.

**THE SHOT LIST HAS NOW BEEN AMENDED TWICE AND BOTH AMENDMENTS ARE KEPT VISIBLE.**

*First,* when the page was one held frame. Act 01 was written as a *reveal* —
position holds, the target drifts. That cannot produce the signature: rotating
the camera swings the car and the word behind it by the same angular amount, so
the occlusion barely changes, and parallax needs translation. Translation was
measured and rejected too — a 1.6 m lateral move slid the car's nose 180 px right
while its tail came 50 px left, which is not a parallax but a second photograph,
and it destroyed a frame placed against measured column edges. So the camera held
and the word travelled instead.

*Then Alex asked for one scene and no fold,* and that reasoning inverted. With
four composed frames rather than one, the same translation is no longer damage —
it is the cut between two shots, and it is the thing that carries the reader from
one to the next. The camera moves again. It simply never moves while anything is
being read.

## 7c · The portrait shots, which are different photographs

Portrait is not the landscape frame made smaller, and it cannot be. three.js
takes a **vertical** FOV, so a 390 × 844 screen has a horizontal field of about
fourteen degrees; fitting a 4.2 m car across it needs seventeen metres of
distance, at which the car occupies a thirteenth of the height — a slab in the
middle of a black page. Measured, not assumed.

Portrait therefore takes the shots a magazine takes on a portrait page — crops,
not reductions, one authored per act:

| Act | Portrait frame | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | the front end alone, filling the width, front wheel outside the frame | — |
| 02 | the flank from the front wheel back, side pipe running out of frame | the wheel enters at 0.11 of the width; a wheel is the fastest scale reference a car has |
| 03 | rear three-quarter, the whole car above the copy | the silhouette occupies 0.271–0.627 of the height, leaving the bottom 37% black |
| 04 | front quarter, standing back | — |

The crop is the composition in every one. Annotations are not projected at this
width (`DNA62`): a leader across a 390 px frame is longer than the part it names.

**Both ends of the frame are flagged now, and the top one only became necessary
once the camera started moving.** With a single held frame the top of the picture
was always the dark ceiling of the room, so the scene number sat on black by
luck. Across four acts the luck runs out — in portrait, act 03 puts the
windscreen exactly where the eyebrow is, and a bone mono label lands on pale
glass. A composition that depends on which way the car happens to be pointing is
not a composition.

## 7b · Mobile shot list, authored separately (`DNA67`, `DNA44`)

Fewer acts and one device. **Act 01 keeps the reveal; acts 02 and 04 lose their
camera entirely** and become static frames — a lateral dolly on a 390px frame
travels four centimetres of car and reads as a jitter. Annotations are not
projected on mobile; they become a list under the frame (`DNA62`).

## 11 · Deviations from the brief, argued rather than absorbed

**Micro type is 14px, not 11–13px.** Alex's brief asks for 11–13px mono labels.
`DNA62` cites `I7` — functional text never below 14px — and `I7` is layer 2, an
invariant. Layer 1 and 2 do not yield to a stated aesthetic preference, so the
floor wins and the label is set at 14px with the tracking doing the work the size
was meant to do. The look the brief wanted survives; the size does not.

**Barlow Condensed replaces Familjen Grotesk as the announcing voice.** That is a
dialect-tier change, which Alex may make, and it is recorded in `index9.css` with
the reason: condensed buys authored line breaks at hero scale and a vertical
rhythm a normal-width grotesque cannot produce.

**Three heroes exist in one file during the pass.** Two of them are cut before
delivery. A page that ships three compositions has none. — **DONE.** A was kept;
B and C are gone from the markup, the stylesheet and the script, and the reason
each was cut is recorded at the top of `scene/index9.js`.

**Oxblood is not a text colour on this page, and pass 1 had it wrong.** The
eyebrow and the three section labels were set in `--oxblood` on the dark ground.
Measured on the composited render that is **1.85 : 1** — not a close call, a
straight AA failure, and `CLAUDE.md` already said why: oxblood is text on light
and **fill** on dark. Every accent is now a filled oxblood mark with a bone label
beside it, which is the same signal at 13.8 : 1.

**The floor was the page's biggest single defect and it was measured, not
judged.** Pass 1 read **#4a4c51 – #5a5c61** off the open floor with a colour
picker — a mid grey two stops above the darkest wall. Roughness went 0.34 → 0.26
and `envMapIntensity` 1.15 → 0.62 so the overhead strip lands as a streak rather
than a wash, and the light pool went from a frame-wide gloss to a small patch.
The open floor now reads **#14171a**.

**That pool was later rebuilt again, and the second version of it was wrong for a
reason worth keeping.** Reasoning from "a pool in front of the car erases its
shadow", it was pushed 1.3–1.7 m away from the lens — which put its far edge
behind the car and its NEAR edge, where the gradient is already zero, exactly
where the visible floor begins. From a low camera the floor you can see is the
floor on your own side of the car. The push is now small, the footprint is
larger, and the shadow is left to the contact map, whose job it is.

**The horizon survived in one last place: the bottom of the frame.** With the
floor dark the render still reached **#6a6a6a** along the hero's bottom row while
the page behind it is **#08090b** — a hundred-level step running the full width
at the section seam. A gradient scrim now takes the frame to the page's own
background exactly, so the seam is zero by construction at every viewport height.

**The scripts-removed page is a second deliverable, not a fallback** — and under
the single-stage grammar it became the *primary* authoring order. The four acts
are written as four ordinary articles in normal document flow; the stage is what
JavaScript does to them. Four readers land in flow and they are not the same
reader: scripts off or a crawler; no WebGL, or a machine too thin; GSAP failed to
load; `prefers-reduced-motion`. All four get four complete compositions in
document order, and the car is not thrown away with the motion — the canvas is
fixed behind them and parked on act 01's frame. Authored stage-first, that reader
would have received four absolutely positioned boxes at opacity zero: a blank
page that validates.

The one thing flow loses is the annotation. A leader points at a part of a car,
and if the reader can scroll the copy away from the car the leader points at
nothing. Annotations belong to the stage and only to the stage.

**Act 03 broke three ways at once and it is worth recording how.** The spec plate
stood in a right-hand column the car's tail already occupied; the heading
laddered into four ragged lines against a 12ch ceiling; and its annotation's
label landed on the headline with a leader crossing the whole frame to the wrong
wheel — from behind the car, the near wheel and the named wheel are not the same
wheel. The plate is now a strip riveted along the foot of the frame, the heading
has a column more and a size less, and the annotation is gone. Two leaders across
four acts is the restraint the frame was asking for.
