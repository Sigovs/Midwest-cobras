Direction

Three lines that settle most arguments before they start.

Anchor — technical-luxury (~70%). Specification is content, not an appendix. Evidence outranks adjectives: where a claim can be made with a figure or a photograph of the real part, it is made that way. It owns composition, grid, hierarchy, type, spacing, colour, geometry, information and interface motion.

Contrast — cinematic-industrial (~30%). Light, depth, material, camera. It owns the canvas and stops at its edge: no type, no grid, no control, no spacing value.

dimensionality: MAIN. The scene carries the dominant, which is why DM1–DM10 bind at full weight rather than as aspirations.

The structural decision specific to this content: the car lives in the dark; its specification lives in the light.

The accent, and the rule attached to it

CONFIGURE
--oxblood #820007 label on it: 10.76:1 — AAA

Sampled from the client's own wordmark, not chosen. When the mark goes silver, the colour does not leave the brand — it moves from the mark into the system.

Oxblood is text on light and fill on dark. It measures 8.55:1 on bone and 2.18:1 on graphite. On the dark ground it is a filled mark carrying a --bone-050 label, and it is never a text colour there. That is a measurement, not a preference.

Remove the accent entirely. If the information stops being classified, the accent was decorating rather than signalling, and the section is wrong.

Type

Three voices, three jobs, and the jobs are what make them three rather than a collection. A fourth has to state the systemic job these cannot do.

Familjen Grotesk — announces

Display only. Headlines, the price when it is the headline, section titles.

Source Sans 3 — informs

All prose. Drawn for technical documentation, which is what most of this site is.

JetBrains Mono — records

Figures, units, labels, spec rows. Never prose. Metrically related to Plex Sans, so a spec strip aligns with the paragraph beside it without nudging.

Body text is set for sustained reading rather than for the screenshot: seventeen pixels, a line height that survives two hundred words, and a measure that stops before the eye loses the return. Prose links inside it stay underlined, which is the opposite rule from navigation.

Section rhythm is deliberately bottom-heavy. --section-pt clamps 64→128, --section-pb clamps 96→192. Reading runs downward, so a mass leans into the space beneath it; a deeper field below gives each section a clean ending, which is what makes a long page read as chapters instead of as a stack.

Geometry

Hairlines and machined edges. A container marks a system boundary, never a decorative frame.

--hairline · 1px · --radius · 2px

One rule weight in the whole system. One radius, and it is small enough to read as a machined corner rather than as a rounded card.

--lift · the only shadow

Reserved for layers that genuinely float — the nav dropdown, the lightbox. A shadow is a claim about elevation; on something that never rises, the eye reads it as mush.

Order of separation, before a box is considered

  1. 1 — whitespace alone
  2. 2 — a hairline
  3. 3 — a background shift with no border
  4. 4 — an actual bordered container, and only then

Motion

Interface motion is precise and short: it confirms a state or reveals a measurement, and then stops. Scene motion is scroll-driven and has no duration of its own — it lives in the scene module, not here.

--dur-1 · 120ms

press, check, focus

--dur-2 · 200ms

hover, small reveal

--dur-3 · 320ms

crossfade, panel

--dur-4 · 520ms

a mass entering

--travel is 8px and it is a ceiling. Content swaps crossfade; they do not slide in from off-screen. Anything that moves further than eight pixels in an interface transition is performing.

Motion binds to roles. [data-motion='rise'] describes what a mass is. A selector naming a specific section animates nothing on the next page and raises no error while doing it — a silent no-op looks exactly like a page where motion was never designed.

Reduced motion is an authored path. Durations collapse, travel goes to zero, every state change still arrives. The hero's reduced-motion answer is a composed still, not the animation stopped on a frame.

Components

Five rules a change has to keep

If a change breaks one of these, the change is wrong — not the rule.

  1. Every value resolves to a token in tokens.css. No hex, no px, no duration below that file. Alpha comes from a channel token, never from a new colour.

  2. Oxblood is text on light and fill on dark. 8.55:1 and 2.18:1 respectively — the second number is why the rule exists.

  3. Ground is set by role, not by section. [data-ground='light'] where facts are read; graphite where the car is looked at. Nothing else changes with it.

  4. Motion binds to [data-motion] roles. The page-load arrival is the one legitimate exception, because a page arrives once.

  5. Every number carries a unit and a ledger entry. A spec row that cannot be sourced is copy, and copy does not go in a spec plate.

What this system does not have

Listed rather than discovered later. An honest gap is cheaper than a surprise.

  • No card. Inventory shows two to four cars; four cards would be one module shown four times. Separation is whitespace, then a hairline, then a ground shift.
  • No icon set yet. None has been earned by repetition. When one is, it is one set, stroke matched to its neighbouring type, and never the only carrier of meaning.
  • No self-hosted fonts yet. Familjen Grotesk, Source Sans 3 and JetBrains Mono load from Google Fonts. Before ship they are self-hosted as woff2 — a third-party request in the critical path is a dependency nobody chose.
  • No form styling. The configurator's controls arrive with the configurator, designed against real option data rather than invented ahead of it.
  • No dark/light toggle. The two grounds are a compositional decision about role, not a user preference, and exposing them as one would make the decision meaningless.
  • No hero asset that can carry the hero. The supplied 3D model is a generic Cobra with SketchUp default materials and a low-polygon body whose faceting shows under any specular light. It is diagram-grade, not hero-grade. See docs/design-read.md §8 — the three honest outcomes, and Alex chooses between them.
