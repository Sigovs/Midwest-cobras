## The direction, in one screen

```
Delivery:        BUILD — direction A of three, selected
Reading:         marque site for a builder-dealer of hand-assembled Cobras,
                 for a buyer commissioning a car, leaning workshop-technical
Mandate:         REDESIGN. Carried: the script wordmark, and its colour #820007
                 moving from the mark into the system. There is no prior site.
Style mode:      HYBRID
  Anchor         technical-luxury  (~70%) — composition, grid, hierarchy, type,
                 spacing, colour, geometry, information, interface motion
  Contrast       cinematic-industrial (~30%) — light, depth, material, camera.
                 It owns the canvas and stops at its edge.
  Signature      none. auction-editorial was considered and dropped: its one
                 candidate device is the spec plate, which the anchor already
                 owns and does better.
Unifying idea:   a specification that has been given light
Dimensionality:  MAIN
```

**The structural decision specific to this content:**
**the car lives in the dark; its specification lives in the light.**
Two grounds, one system. `[data-ground='light']` re-points the semantic aliases
and changes nothing else. Split by role, never by section.

**Why the palette is what it is.** Your own corpus already holds lux-cars
(`#0B0B0D`, red), 360-auto-care (`#090c0d`, orange, Archivo) and hinderer
(`#1f1d19`, bronze). A fourth near-black car site with a red accent is the
repeat that `projects:check` exists to catch. Hence graphite `#1A1D21` — cooler
and lighter than all three — an accent sampled from the client's own wordmark,
and three voices that collide with none of your other projects.

### The scroll effect, as built

- **Arrival on load.** The car comes to a stop from far, high and turned away.
  Bound to this page by name — a page arrives once, and that is the only
  legitimate by-name binding. It gates nothing.
- **Rotation on scroll.** One scrubbed timeline across the reveal. Scrub means
  the timeline is a function of scroll position: stop and the car stops, scroll
  back and it rewinds. The reader keeps the transport.
- **Four notes, one per screen, one fact each** — RT4 from $66,900 · RT4B from
  $70,500 · ceramic-coated side pipes and roll bars · 18-inch rims. All sourced.
  Fading, deliberately not scrubbed: a caption at 43% opacity because someone
  stopped between screens is a frame that cannot be read.
- **Poses live in the markup**, not in the timeline — `data-rot`, `data-cam`,
  `data-target`, `data-fov`, with `-mobile` variants for the separately authored
  narrow camera. Add a note in HTML and it gets choreography for free.
- **Every exit leaves the page complete.** Reduced motion, no WebGL, three cores
  or fewer, under 3 GB, data-saver: the scene never starts, the authored still
  stays, and the notes render as one plate with every fact present.

### The asset verdict, with evidence rather than opinion

`COBRA.obj` is a SketchUp export whose **stored normals are inverted** — a
reversed face looks correct inside SketchUp, so the error ships silently. The
first render looked like a hollow shell. `dev/obj2glb.py` recomputes normals at
a 62° crease and the geometry underneath turns out to be a sound, recognisable
Cobra: nose, grille, side pipes, roll bar, spoked wheels.

But the body is low-polygon enough that faceting reads at hero scale under any
specular light. **Diagram-grade, not hero-grade.** And it is a generic Cobra,
not a Backdraft RT4. The hero still committed here is a **declared placeholder**;
the composition is the deliverable, the asset is not.

## Direction E — index5.html (2026-08-25)

Built from `donot git/prompt-final.md`. Four new files, one shared file amended.

**New**
- `assets/js/scene/parts.js` — the manifest. Ten steps as a table: id, label, node
  prefixes, and the axis each part arrives along. Separated from the motion so
  re-ordering the build never means touching a timeline. `resolveParts()` returns
  flights (a symmetric pair is one flight) and reports any prefix it cannot find.
- `assets/js/scene/assembly5.js` — plays the manifest. Owns timing only. At most
  three flights in the air; the windows are sized from that number rather than
  chosen. Every frame is computed from `p` alone, so scrubbing back to 0 is an
  empty stage by construction.
- `assets/js/scene/index5.js` — the entry. One held frame, the strike, the
  derived captions.
- `index5.html` — ten `[data-step]` captions, lanes grouped into three
  compositions.

**Reused unchanged:** `stage.css`, `lower.js`, `lamps.js`, `hero-scene.js`.
Directions A–D are untouched and index4 was re-checked live after the one shared
edit below.

**Amended:** `lanes.js` — `makeOverlapAssert` now measures VISIBLE geometry only.
`Box3.setFromObject` traverses hidden children, so on an assembly sequence it was
measuring parts parked at their flight offsets and reporting overlaps against a
car that was not on screen yet. It fired twice on the first run of index5 for
exactly that reason. Rendering is unchanged on every direction; only the
assertion got more accurate.

**Verified against the GLB, not against the prompt.** All 31 node prefixes in the
manifest resolve: 36 groups across 10 steps, 0 missing. Two things the check
turned up:
- `ac_cobra_kit00_chassis` matches two nodes (`_cpit` and `_loda`). Both belong to
  step 1, which has no flight, so this is harmless.
- There is exactly ONE mirror node, `ac_cobra_kit00_mirror_l_cpit`. The caption
  was written as "Glass and mirrors" and has been corrected to the singular in
  both the manifest and the page. The car has one mirror.

**Two claims in prompt-final.md that were already stale when it was written**, and
are recorded here so they are not re-derived wrongly a third time:
- "the renderer has alpha" — it did not, until it was fixed on 2026-08-24.
- "every part is reachable via `car.getObjectByName`" — half true. The transforms
  live on parent GROUPS; mesh nodes sit at the origin carrying the material name
  as a suffix. Animating the mesh node collapses all four wheels onto the origin.

**The locked-pose question the prompt asked to have flagged.** The frame is a
front three-quarter — `rotY 32°, cam [0.30, 1.02, 7.85], target [0, 0.48, 0],
fov 31`. From it the headlamps face the viewer and the tail lamps face away, and
with a locked camera there is no pose where both ends face you. The tails are
therefore not spent on a surface nobody sees: they come up at 0.58 and what reads
is their reflection in the floor behind the car, which the reflector already
draws. Confirmed on screen at full strike — a red presence at the rear, smaller
than the heads, which is what one end of a car lit away from you looks like.

**Not done.** The wheels still do not rotate — all four share merged primitives.
The exhaust has no separate node; the pipes are welded into the chassis mesh, so
there is no "pipes" step and the manifest does not pretend otherwise.
